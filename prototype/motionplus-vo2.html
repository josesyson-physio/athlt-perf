Getting the most accurate number

  • Direct gas analysis (Tier A) is the only true measurement. Portable analysers bring it to the track or clinic.
  • Without gas analysis, a maximal-effort field test (Tier B) beats submaximal tests, which assume everyone's max heart rate follows an age formula.
  • Use a chest strap or a careful 15-second manual count for test heart rate. Wrist and phone-camera sensors drift during and right after exercise.
  • Measure max heart rate in a hard test once and enter it. A predicted max HR alone can be off by 10 to 20 bpm.
  • Retest with the same test, course, time of day and warm-up. Changes smaller than the method's error may not be real.
  • Only use an equation inside the group it was built on. The app flags age and sex outside that range.

Built-in safeguards

  • Exercise tests stay locked until screening and consent are recorded. Any yes answer needs a clinician's sign-off before Proceed is allowed.
  • Missing inputs are never guessed. An early stop makes the result invalid instead of scoring it anyway.
  • Each result is labelled Estimated, Invalid or Not available, with the equation, version and source.
  • No "excellent / poor" ratings or percentiles. Those need a matched, cited reference group, which comes later.

Fairness and accuracy

  • Most equations were built on narrow groups (Cooper: US Air Force men). The app flags age and sex outside that group.
  • Optical heart-rate sensors and phone-camera pulse readings lose accuracy with motion and can read worse on darker skin. A manual pulse count or chest strap is preferred for test HR.
  • Non-exercise estimates underrate very fit athletes and overrate inactive people.
  • Claims of accuracy need our own validation against lab testing before launch.

Privacy

  • This prototype has no server. Saved records stay in this browser and can be deleted in one click.
  • Collect the minimum: an athlete ID instead of a name, age instead of date of birth.
  • Health data rules (GDPR, HIPAA, India's DPDP Act and others) apply once data leaves the device. The full app needs consent records, role-based access and an audit trail.
